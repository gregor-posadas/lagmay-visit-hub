When the Waters Rise

Three leading Filipino scholars, one conversation: why does the Philippines keep flooding, who pays for it, and what can people here in the Bay Area do? Dr. Mahar Lagmay, the scientist behind the country's national flood hazard maps, joins Berkeley's Dr. Lisandro Claudio, a prize-winning historian of the Philippine state and its money, and Dr. Diana Martinez, an architectural historian of how the United States built its rule in the islands in concrete.

The speakers

Science, history and the built world: each brings a different answer to why the water keeps rising, and what we owe each other when it does.

  • Dr. Mahar Lagmay

    Executive Director, UP Resilience Institute, and Professor at the UP National Institute of Geological Sciences

    Dr. Lagmay leads Project NOAH, which mapped flood, landslide and storm-surge hazards across the whole Philippines and made them free for anyone to use. The flood maps on this page are his team's. A Cambridge-trained geologist, he was the first Asian scientist to receive the European Geosciences Union's Plinius Medal for natural-hazards research, in 2015. In the national debate over flood control, he has argued that new concrete should be the last resort, not the first.

    Selected work

    • Project NOAH (opens in a new tab), the national hazard maps
    • Devastating storm surges of Typhoon Haiyan (opens in a new tab), 2015
    • Disseminating near-real-time hazards information and flood maps in the Philippines through Web-GIS (opens in a new tab), 2017

  • Dr. Lisandro Claudio

    Associate Professor of Southeast Asian Studies and Chair of the Center for Southeast Asia Studies, UC Berkeley

    Dr. Claudio is a historian of the modern Philippines: its politics, its ideas and its money. His newest book, The Profligate Colonial, shows how the United States exported austerity to the Philippines, shaping what the colonial state could and could not build. His Liberalism and the Postcolony won the Association for Asian Studies' George McT. Kahin Prize. Before Berkeley he taught at Ateneo de Manila and De La Salle.

    Books

    • The Profligate Colonial: How the US Exported Austerity to the Philippines (opens in a new tab), Cornell, 2025
    • Liberalism and the Postcolony: Thinking the State in 20th-Century Philippines (opens in a new tab), NUS Press, 2017
    • Jose Rizal: Liberalism and the Paradox of Coloniality (opens in a new tab), Palgrave Macmillan, 2019

  • Dr. Diana Martinez

    Assistant Professor of Architecture, College of Environmental Design, UC Berkeley

    Dr. Martinez is an architectural historian of American empire. Her book Concrete Colonialism shows how the United States used concrete, infrastructure and city planning to build its rule over the Philippines. Her next book traces the colonial roots of urban renewal, from Daniel Burnham's 1905 plan for Manila to American cities. She returned to Berkeley, where she studied architecture as an undergraduate, after directing the architectural studies program at Tufts.

    Selected work

    • Concrete Colonialism: Architecture, Urbanism, and the US Imperial Project in the Philippines (opens in a new tab), Duke, 2025
    • Nation Building in the Philippines and the Racial Ordering of International Architecture (opens in a new tab), Grey Room, 2024
