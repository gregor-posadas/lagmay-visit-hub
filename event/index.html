When the Waters Rise

Three leading Filipino scholars, one conversation: why does the Philippines keep flooding, who pays for it, and what can people here in the Bay Area do? Dr. Mahar Lagmay, the scientist behind the country's national flood hazard mapsMaps of where water would go, and how deep, if a given rain fell. Project NOAH's maps show this for rains so heavy they come about once every 5, 25 or 100 years., joins Berkeley's Dr. Lisandro Claudio, a prize-winning historian of the Philippine state and its money, and Dr. Diana Martinez, an architectural historian of how the United States built its rule in the islands in concrete.

The speakers

Science, history and the built world: each brings a different answer to why the water keeps rising, and what we owe each other when it does. Why we put these three on one stage.

  • Dr. Mahar Lagmay

    Executive Director, UP Resilience InstituteA University of the Philippines institute that studies disasters and climate risk and helps communities prepare. It runs Project NOAH., and Professor at the UP National Institute of Geological Sciences

    Dr. Lagmay leads Project NOAHNationwide Operational Assessment of Hazards. Launched in 2012 under the Department of Science and Technology, with Dr. Lagmay as its head, it maps floods, landslides and storm surges for the whole country. The University of the Philippines has run it since 2017., which mapped flood, landslide and storm-surgeA storm surge is seawater pushed ashore by a typhoon's winds. Yolanda's surge in 2013 rose several metres in Tacloban. hazards across the whole Philippines and made them free for anyone to use. The flood maps on this page are his team's. A Cambridge-trained geologist, he was the first Asian scientist to receive the European Geosciences Union's Plinius MedalThe European Geosciences Union's medal for interdisciplinary research on natural hazards., in 2015. In the national debate over flood control, he has argued that new concrete should be the last resort, not the first.

    aalagmay@up.edu.ph

    Selected work

    • Project NOAH, the national hazard maps
    • Devastating storm surges of Typhoon Haiyan, 2015
    • Disseminating near-real-time hazards information and flood maps in the Philippines through Web-GIS, 2017

  • Dr. Lisandro Claudio

    Associate Professor of Southeast Asian Studies and Chair of the Center for Southeast Asia Studies, UC Berkeley

    Dr. Claudio is a historian of the modern Philippines: its politics, its ideas and its money. His newest book, The Profligate Colonial, shows how the United States exported austerityKeeping government spending tight, even on things a country needs. Dr. Claudio traces how American colonial rule ran the Philippines on a tight budget, and what that meant for what the state could build. to the Philippines, shaping what the colonial state could and could not build. His Liberalism and the Postcolony won the Association for Asian Studies' George McT. Kahin Prize. Before Berkeley he taught at Ateneo de Manila and De La Salle.

    lclaudio@berkeley.edu

    Books

    • The Profligate Colonial: How the US Exported Austerity to the Philippines, Cornell, 2025
    • Liberalism and the Postcolony: Thinking the State in 20th-Century Philippines, NUS Press, 2017
    • Jose Rizal: Liberalism and the Paradox of Coloniality, Palgrave Macmillan, 2019

  • Dr. Diana Martinez

    Assistant Professor of Architecture, College of Environmental Design, UC Berkeley

    Dr. Martinez is an architectural historian of American empire. Her book Concrete Colonialism shows how the United States used concrete, infrastructure and city planning to build its rule over the Philippines. Her next book traces the colonial roots of urban renewalPrograms, mostly in mid-20th-century American cities, that cleared older and often poorer neighbourhoods to rebuild them., from Daniel BurnhamThe Chicago architect and planner who drew up plans for Manila and Baguio in 1905, when the Philippines was an American colony.'s 1905 plan for Manila to American cities. She returned to Berkeley, where she studied architecture as an undergraduate, after directing the architectural studies program at Tufts.

    dianajmartinez@berkeley.edu

    Selected work

    • Concrete Colonialism: Architecture, Urbanism, and the US Imperial Project in the Philippines, Duke, 2025
    • Nation Building in the Philippines and the Racial Ordering of International Architecture, Grey Room, 2024

The same morning, Dr. Lagmay also gives a guest lecture in Development Engineering 203, Digital Transformation of Development, a graduate course in Berkeley's Development EngineeringA Berkeley field that designs, tests and studies technology with and for communities in low-resource settings, from water systems to digital tools. program.

About the map, your answers and the data

  • Your name and email are only for the RSVP list. They never appear here or at the event.
  • The map shows counts by Bay Area county and Philippine province. If you pick "Prefer not to say", you are counted but not placed.
  • If you share a flood story and say yes to sharing it, a teammate reads it first. A few may be read aloud at the event, never with a name.
  • The blue is where a 100-year rain would flood, shaded by depth. Guimaras, Siquijor, Sulu and Tawi-Tawi have no NOAH 100-year map in this set, and some provinces are only partly mapped. At the event, the map zooms into a few towns to show every building and street.
