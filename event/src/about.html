When the Waters Rise

About the event

Why we are bringing a scientist and two historians together to talk about floods, why here, and who we are.

Why this conversation

Floods in the Philippines are usually told as an engineering story. Rain falls, rivers rise, and the fix is a higher wall or a deeper drain. That story is true, but it leaves out most of what decides who ends up under water.

Which towns get mapped, which get protected and which get concrete is settled by budgets, politics and choices that go back more than a century. In 2025 the country saw this plainly when billions of pesos meant for flood control went to projects that were poorly built or never built at all, and tens of thousands marched in protest. A flood is a natural hazard. Who it hurts is a human decision.

So this event puts three fields on one stage.

  • The science. Dr. Lagmay has spent his career showing where water will go, and making those maps free so that a mayor, a planner or a family can use them before the storm.
  • The state and its money. Dr. Claudio traces how the Philippine state was built to run on too little, and what that has meant for what it can build and maintain.
  • The built world. Dr. Martinez shows how American rule used concrete and city planning to remake the islands, and how those plans still shape who lives where the water comes.

None of these fields can explain the floods alone, and none can fix them alone. We want engineers to hear the historians, historians to hear the scientist, and everyone in the room to leave with a fuller picture than any one of them could give.

Why here

The Bay Area is home to one of the largest Filipino communities in the United States, and many people here have family in towns that flood almost every year. For them, a typhoon is not a headline but a phone call home. We want this to be a place where that worry meets the people who study it, and where people with no ties to the Philippines can see why it matters to their neighbours.

Who is organizing it

We are three Filipino graduate students at Berkeley from three different fields. Gregor Posadas studies water governance in Civil and Environmental Engineering. Noam Anglo is in Development EngineeringA Berkeley field that designs, tests and studies technology with and for communities in low-resource settings.. Both work on BahaWatch, a low-cost flood-sensing project. Veronica Sison is a historian in Southeast Asian Studies who studies the history of technocratic thoughtThe idea that trained experts, like engineers and economists, should make a country's big decisions. in the Philippines. Our team already looks like the conversation we hope to have. Rapha Felipe at Stanford leads the Stanford side of the visit, where Dr. Lagmay also plans to speak, and is helping find support for his trip. She worked on flood risk mapping across the Philippines at the Asian Infrastructure Investment BankA development bank, founded in 2016, that lends money for roads, power, water and flood protection across Asia. and now works on disaster risk in Stanford's Baker Lab.
